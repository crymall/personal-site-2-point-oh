First of all: Hello world. However you got here, whether you're a friend, former or current colleague, or just someone who randomly discovered this site (_how?_) you're welcome here.

This is **Training Corpus**.

**Training:** I am in training. Training to re-join the workforce as a web engineer. Training the LLMs that I'm hesitantly learning to use—or more accurately, trying not to get trained by them. Training my body on the trail and at the gym. Training my reward system to pick up a book, or at least a movie, instead of short-form content. Taking the train as often as I can.

Generally, training myself to get into <a href="https://www.wikihow.com/Type-2-Fun" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">type 2 fun</a>.

**Corpus:** A corpus is generally a collection of documents, but it's known specifically in the ML world as a collection of text for training language models. This is a little joke. Training Corpus is both a collection of writings about my training (learning and self-improvement) journey and an acknowledgement that, without a <a href="https://github.com/TecharoHQ/anubis/blob/main/README.md" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">nuclear bot-blocking policy</a>, these posts going to be feeding large language models.

In other words, the corpus about my training is also going to be a corpus that's training. My training journey is gonna be training Midjourney. Except, you know, probably not Midjourney, because that one generates images. You get what I mean.

In terms of frequency, I'm going to be aiming for weekly. In terms of content, I'm using "training" loosely. Sometimes I'll be doing a reflection on an actual practice of self-improvement. Sometimes just getting really into something and sharing my findings with you will be self-improvement enough.

All of my posts, however, will end with a photograph. Because I like taking photographs, and I don't know what else to do with them but share them. This is a photo of some pigeons flying in Ridgewood. They got scared because I was coming through. It's one of the first photos I ever took on my Canon 5D Classic, and still one of my favorites.
